# What is the difference between a managed Power Platform Environment and a unmanaged Power Platform Environment?

In Microsoft Power Platform, environments are isolated containers for apps, flows, 
Dataverse tables, connections, and solutions. They define access control, data governance, 
and ALM (Application Lifecycle Management) boundaries. 

The distinction between Managed and Unmanaged environments is crucial for governance and 
deployment strategies.

## Managed Environments

Managed Environments are governance-focused, production-ready spaces with strict controls. 

They:

- Allow only managed solutions (locked, non-editable components)
- Enforce solution checker, data loss prevention policies, and sharing restrictions
- Provide maker operation reporting, AI-powered insights, and environment routing
- Prevent direct edits to components, ensuring stability and compliance

Are ideal for production, UAT, and controlled test environments.

---

## Unmanaged Environments

Unmanaged Environments are flexible, development-friendly spaces. 

They:

- Allow unmanaged solutions (editable, source of truth for ALM)
- Enable creation of new tables, flows, and app components
- Have minimal governance, making them suitable for development, sandbox, and training
- Support experimentation and rapid prototyping without impacting production.

---

## Why unmanaged solutions are blocked in Managed Environments: 

Unmanaged solutions permit direct edits, schema changes, and uncontrolled modifications, 
which can destabilize production systems. Managed environments require all changes to 
originate from a development environment, be exported as managed, and then imported.

## Typical ALM Flow:

- Development Environment (Unmanaged) – Build and test components in unmanaged solutions.
- Export as Managed Solution – Lock components for deployment.
- Import into Managed Environment – No direct edits allowed; updates must come from Dev.

## Key Differences:

- Governance: Minimal in unmanaged vs. strongest in managed.
- Maker Freedom: High in unmanaged, low in managed.
- Production Safety: Low in unmanaged, very high in managed.
- Solution Type: Unmanaged in dev; managed in test/UAT/prod.

## Best Practice 

Use unmanaged environments for building and iterating, and managed environments 
for secure, stable deployments with full governance controls. This ensures repeatable 
releases, auditability, and reduced production risks.
